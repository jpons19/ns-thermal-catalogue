# Literature audit for neutron-star cooling comparisons

**14 November 2026 · Rotation-powered pulsars and X-ray isolated neutron stars · B2334+61 discussion updated 18 November 2026**

This review covers the 40 entries in these classes in the project, including the pulsars with upper limits and the hot-spot comparison group. The detailed cases below were checked against the cited papers and the original tables where available. The source register also includes cases reviewed through the published compilation. Catalogue selections remain provisional.

**Conventions.** Luminosities below are redshifted bolometric luminosities in erg/s unless a band is specified. Spectral colour temperatures and atmosphere effective temperatures refer to their fitted components. Age from proper motion depends on the inferred birthplace. A remnant age may also depend on distance, expansion history or an assumed association. The characteristic age τc = P/(2Ṗ) follows from current timing. The difference between these estimators is generally a physical or model-dependent difference. A failure of tabulated P and Ṗ to reproduce the tabulated τc is a numerical inconsistency. At fixed spectral model and unabsorbed bolometric flux, L∞ scales as d².

**Reference keys.** [V13 — Viganò et al. 2013, Tables 1 and 3](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stt1008) · [P20 — Potekhin et al. 2020, Tables 1 and 2 and §3.3](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/staa1871) · [Web — Potekhin compilation, 3 August 2026 snapshot](https://www.ioffe.ru/astro/NSG/thermal/cooldat.html) · [M24 — Marino et al. 2024, Extended Data Tables 1–2 and Supplementary Data Table 6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-024-02291-y). The web table often inherits the P20 spectral analysis or adopts a named later analysis. Its rows and P20 therefore often represent the same evidence.

## 1. PSR J0205+6449 · 3C 58

- **Age provenance.** P20 uses 819 yr at the spectral epoch and M24 gives 841 yr in its main text. Both rely on the association with SN 1181. Kothes supports that association along with a distance of 2.0 ± 0.3 kpc. Ritter et al. identify Pa 30 with SN 1181. Radio expansion of 3C 58 gives a much longer constant-expansion timescale, roughly 7 kyr, which is sensitive to the expansion history. With the P and Ṗ in M24 Extended Data Table 1, τc = **5.38 kyr**, versus 58.8 kyr in that table and 54 kyr in the Methods. The spin-down value and the radio expansion timescale have different physical assumptions. [Kothes 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.8384) · [Ritter et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/ac2253) · [Bietenholz 2006](https://doi.org/10.1086/504584) · [M24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05371)
- **Thermal alternatives at 2.0 kpc.** M24 finds a BB+PL fit with kT∞ = 200 (+80/−40) eV, nominal BB radius 0.6 km and hot-spot L∞ = 1.44 (+0.60/−0.50) × 10³². Its additional 11 km blackbody gives Lcool < 10²⁸ under that fit. P20 reanalyses Chandra with NSMAXG+PL and fixes M = 1.4 M☉ and R = 13 km. It gives a whole-surface kT∞ near 49 eV and L∞ = 1.9 (+1.5/−1.1) × 10³². The detected components have overlapping bolometric luminosities and very different temperatures and emitting areas. The M24 limit tests an extra cool blackbody conditional on the hot-spot model. [M24, Methods and tables](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05371) · [P20, object 14](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15004)
- **Decision for discussion.** Which age scenarios to carry, and whether to retain the two spectral decompositions as alternatives. The age and decomposition each change what the star can say about fast cooling.

## 2. PSR B2334+61 · G114.3+0.3

- **Age and distance are coupled.** Yar-Uyaniker et al. estimate **0.7 kpc from H I** and derive about **7.7 kyr** using a pressure-driven snowplow model for the remnant at that distance. The age calculation assumes an ambient density and remnant radius. M24 uses this 7.7 kyr with an adopted 10% analysis uncertainty and independently uses the older **3.1 (+0.2/−1.0) kpc** NE2001 dispersion-measure estimate for the pulsar. P20 quotes 2.08 kpc from YMW17. A self-consistent remnant-age estimate at the larger distance remains open. The 40–41 kyr characteristic age is a separate estimator. [Yar-Uyaniker et al. 2004, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0408386) · [M24, Methods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05371) · [P20, object 33](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15004)
- **One XMM observation, several fits.** McGowan et al. find a BB at T∞ ≈ 1.62 MK with a small emitting area. Their magnetized hydrogen NSA alternatives fix M = 1.4 M☉ and R = 10 or 13 km and infer **1.1 ± 0.6** or **3.2 ± 1.7 kpc**, respectively. These distances are fit normalizations, with radius and spectrum assumptions. M24 freezes N_H at 2 × 10²¹ cm⁻² in its low-count BB fit, obtains kT∞ = 160 ± 10 eV and quotes hot-spot Lobs = 0.78 (+0.30/−0.10) × 10³² and Lstat = 0.60 (+0.49/−0.36) × 10³². Those are different summaries. At fixed M24 bolometric flux, 1.1 kpc gives about 9.8 × 10³⁰, and 0.7 kpc about 4.0 × 10³⁰. Those scalings apply to the M24 blackbody and preserve its assumed spectrum. [McGowan et al. 2006, §4 and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0508439) · [M24, Extended Data Table 2 and Supplementary Data Table 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05371)
- **Possible extra redshift in P20.** McGowan et al. expressly describe the ~0.58 and ~0.65 MK NSA temperatures as measured at Earth. P20 describes them as local, obtaining 38 and 46 eV after a redshift conversion. Taking the primary paper’s labels literally gives about **50 and 56 eV**. For the 10 km model, using 0.58 MK as T∞ in 4πR∞²σT∞⁴ gives **1.37 × 10³²**, versus **4.73 × 10³¹** if 0.58 MK is local. For the 13 km model the analogous values are **3.15 × 10³²** and **1.47 × 10³²**. The latter pair of local-temperature calculations reproduces P20’s luminosities. These are consistency calculations at the central parameters, with no refit. The fluxes in McGowan Table 1 are **0.3–10 keV**, so direct conversion of those fluxes alone cannot settle the bolometric discrepancy. XSPEC's NSA input temperature is local. McGowan's footnote says the temperature in the table is obtained after conversion. The original fit parameters or a reproduction of the fit would establish which values were actually tabulated. [McGowan et al. 2006, footnote 2 and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0508439) · [P20, object 33](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15004) · [XSPEC NSA documentation](https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/software/xspec/manual/node236.html)
- **Selection history.** The web value at 1.1 kpc inherits P20’s temperature-convention question. The working decisions below supersede the earlier unrestricted set of alternatives.

### Discussion record and working decisions · 18 November 2026

**Status: discussion closed for now; distance adopted, luminosity envelope provisional, age unresolved.** This record supersedes the open selection question above. Earlier calculations remain as provenance, not additional accepted branches.

#### Distance and luminosity

- **Adopt M24's distance, d = 3.1 (+0.2/−1.0) kpc.** Exclude the nearby-distance branches from the current working luminosity envelope. Compare P20 and M24 only; do not count the original McGowan results as additional independent alternatives. P20's atmosphere entries inherit those fits, so the provenance remains documented.
- **Distinguish tabulated distance from spectral normalization.** P20 Table 1 lists approximately 0.7 kpc from the remnant H I study. Its adopted L∞ = (4.7 ± 3.5) × 10³¹ erg/s is instead calculated from the canonical 10 km NSA fit, whose inferred distance is 1.1 ± 0.6 kpc. P20 does not rescale that luminosity to the 0.7 kpc table entry. P20 also discusses a 13 km atmosphere solution with inferred d = 3.2 ± 1.7 kpc and L∞ = 1.5 (+1.0/−1.2) × 10³² erg/s. The latter is compatible with the adopted distance and is retained as the working atmosphere alternative; this compatibility is not a new fit constrained to M24's narrower distance interval. P20 also mentions the YMW17 DM distance of 2.08 kpc. [P20, Tables 1–2 and §3.3 object 33](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15004)
- **M24 supplies a separate spectral reanalysis of the same XMM observation.** Its BB fit has kT∞ = 160 ± 10 eV and Rbb ≈ 0.9 km, with N_H fixed at 2 × 10²¹ cm⁻². At the adopted distance its reported Lobs = 7.8 (+3.0/−1.0) × 10³¹ erg/s and propagated Lstat = 6.0 (+4.9/−3.6) × 10³¹ erg/s are different summaries; the latter interval is [2.4 × 10³¹, 1.09 × 10³²] erg/s. Both quoted intervals are 90%. Distance and flux uncertainty already enter Lstat; do not apply distance endpoints to it a second time. [M24, Extended Data Table 2, Supplementary Data Table 6 and Methods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05371)
- **Numerical overlap does not establish the same physical interpretation.** M24's preferred BB describes a small emitting region, whereas the P20 alternatives assume whole-surface hydrogen-atmosphere emission. M24 also tests NSMAXG atmospheres with H, C and O, fixed distance and B = 10¹² G; acceptable fits have emitting fractions below unity. These do not reproduce or validate the P20 whole-surface NSA assumptions at B = 10¹³ G. The inherited temperature-redshift convention issue remains unresolved; no new branch based directly on McGowan's labels is included in the current envelope. [M24, Methods and Extended Data Table 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05371) · [P20, object 33](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15004)
- **Working luminosity envelope shown in the updated plot:** [2.4 × 10³¹, 2.5 × 10³²] erg/s, taking the extrema of M24's Lstat interval and P20's distance-compatible 13 km atmosphere interval. M24's Lobs = 7.8 × 10³¹ is the displayed fiducial marker. P20's interval is 1σ and M24's is 90%; the combined envelope has no common confidence level. This is an outer comparison across spectral interpretations, not a finalized likelihood or a demonstrated interval for the total cooling luminosity. M24's conditional hidden whole-surface BB limit Lcool < 4 × 10³¹ erg/s remains a separate one-sided constraint and is not added to this box.
- **Excluded scaling illustration.** Multiplying the original P20 point by (3.1/1.1)² would give 3.73 × 10³² erg/s, a factor 7.94 increase. This fixes spectral shape while changing the normalization/emitting area. It is not a valid new fit with the original fixed stellar radius and atmosphere parameters. The purple point in the diagnostic plot is therefore a conditional extrapolation and is excluded from the shaded envelope. Its illustrative error endpoints do not retain a common confidence level or known covariance. The old broad box spanning nearby-distance branches and direct McGowan redshift-label alternatives is superseded.

#### Remnant distance and age

- **Conflicting remnant-distance scenarios.** Older work placed G114.3+0.3 near 3.4 kpc by associating it with H I at roughly −35 to −45 km/s. Yar-Uyaniker et al. associate it with nearby H I near −6.5 km/s, placing it near 0.7 kpc. They additionally cite optical filaments, 21 cm polarization and the relative placement of an H II region as evidence favouring a nearby remnant. They argue that local ionized material can affect the pulsar's DM and RM. These are model and association arguments, rather than competing precise parallax measurements. We retain the discrepancy in the audit despite adopting M24's distance for the current comparison. [Yar-Uyaniker et al. 2004, §§1.1 and 4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0408386)
- **The 7.7 kyr age belongs to the nearby scenario.** Yar-Uyaniker et al. use d ≈ 0.7 kpc, mean radius ≈ 7 pc, ambient density ≈ 14 cm⁻³ and explosion energy 10⁵¹ erg. Their Cioffi-based pressure-driven snowplow calculation gives about 7.7 kyr. The density is itself inferred using assumptions about the remnant shell's contribution to DM/RM, compression and ionization. This is not a directly measured expansion age or a model-independent kinematic clock. [Yar-Uyaniker et al. 2004, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0408386)
- **M24 combines this remnant age with the larger DM distance.** It adopts 7.7 kyr, explicitly assigning 10% relative uncertainty because the original study supplied no error. That ±0.8 kyr does not quantify systematic uncertainty from distance, ambient density, explosion energy or evolutionary phase. At 3.1 kpc the same angular shell would have mean radius ≈ 31 pc, from 7 × (3.1/0.7). This radius scaling is a geometrical calculation; no revised dynamical age has been derived here. The density and dynamics need joint reassessment rather than a blind rescaling of the age. [M24, Methods §Distances and real age constraints and Extended Data Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05371)
- **Timing provides an alternative estimator.** P20 gives τc ≈ 40.6 kyr and M24 ≈ 41.1 kyr. Their difference from 7.7 kyr reflects assumptions about birth period, braking history and remnant evolution; τc is not automatically a measured true age or a rigorous upper bound.
- **Working plotting convention, not an adopted age interval.** The displayed horizontal span 6.93–41.1 kyr retains M24's 7.7 kyr minus its assigned 10% through its characteristic age, solely for continuity of the discussion plot. We have not established these as physical bounds at d = 3.1 kpc. The remnant association and its distance evidence must remain attached to any future remnant-age scenario. Source classification: thermal emission detected; adopted distance is a review choice; spectral interpretation and self-consistent cooling age remain model dependent.

**Next time this object is reopened:** reassess the remnant age at the adopted distance (including density and evolutionary phase), and establish the cooling-luminosity interpretation before replacing the provisional box with a final catalogue interval. No further decision is required in the present discussion.

## 3. Vela pulsar · PSR B0833−45

- **Age.** V13’s log age interval corresponds to about 5–16 kyr. P20 itself differs internally: Table 2 gives 17–23 kyr, while §3.3 adopts 17–27 kyr. The project P20 row follows the table, and the web row follows the text. The timing value is 11.3 kyr. P20 discusses why the long-term braking index and the pre-glitch value imply different timing estimates. [V13, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.2156) · [P20, object 21](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15004)
- **Luminosity.** V13 gives (1.26–2.00) × 10³². At nearly the same distance, Manzali et al. give 3.1 (+0.5/−0.4) × 10³² for 2BB and (2.61 ± 0.06) × 10³² for NSA+PL at fixed R = 10 km. The Ofengeim & Zyuzin NSMAX-based fit at M = 1.4 M☉, R = 13 km gives T∞ = 0.700 ± 0.005 MK and P20 derives (4.24 ± 0.12) × 10³². Across the mass–radius grid Ofengeim & Zyuzin obtain 0.66 (+0.04/−0.01) MK. The narrow luminosity errors for one fixed atmosphere fit do not describe the spread across assumptions. [P20, object 21](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15004) · [Ofengeim & Zyuzin 2018, §3](https://www.ioffe.ru/astro/Stars/Paper/ofengeim_zyuzin18.pdf)
- **Decision adopted on 18 November.** Use the expanded age and luminosity ranges documented below; retain individual fits as provenance rather than independent observations.

### Age decision and causes of the spread · 18 November 2026

**Adopted working age envelope: 11–40 kyr.** This is the review's selected range encompassing the main younger and older age scenarios. It has no statistical confidence level and is not a rigorous bound on all published models. It deliberately replaces V13's 5–16 kyr bracket and the narrower P20 compilation intervals for the current review. No central age has been selected here. The luminosity decision is recorded below.

- **Compilation versus independent evidence.** V13 Table 1 reports log10(t/yr) = 3.7–4.2, or about 5–16 kyr, labelled SNR. Its 5 kyr endpoint is not retained in the adopted range. P20 Table 2 gives 17–23 kyr, whereas its object discussion selects 17–27 kyr. This is an internal mismatch, not two independent age measurements. The discrepancy is retained for provenance. [V13, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.2156) · [P20, §3.3 object 21 and Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15004)
- **Remnant morphology and ejecta dynamics.** P20 summarizes Aschenbach et al. (1995) as 18 ± 9 kyr from extended X-ray features and notes an independent approximately 18 kyr estimate by Jenkins, Silk & Wallerstein (1976). Aschenbach (2002) compares the angular offset of silicon-rich fragment A, about 5.3 degrees from the inferred explosion centre, with velocities expected from progenitor/explosion models. At 250 pc and 11.4 kyr the implied mean speed is about 2000 km/s, higher than the relevant model silicon velocities. This favours an older remnant. The source describes the age as at least roughly 17–23 kyr, rather than measuring a bounded interval with 23 kyr as an upper limit. Distance, explosion centre, progenitor structure, ejecta mixing and deceleration are systematic assumptions. A direct expansion clock was not established by that argument. [Aschenbach 2002, §2.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0208492) · [P20](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15004)
- **Characteristic age.** tau_c = P/(2 Pdot) ≈ 11.3 kyr is a timing estimator assuming constant dipole braking (n=3), a constant torque coefficient and a negligible birth period. It is not a direct determination of elapsed time.
- **Glitch-recovery treatment.** Akbal et al. (2017) extrapolate timing fits to rates just before successive glitches and infer n = 2.81 ± 0.12, giving about 12.5 kyr if extrapolated as a constant braking law with negligible birth period. This is not simply an instantaneous pre-glitch index. Espinoza, Lyne & Stappers (2017) use a different treatment of cumulative glitch effects and obtain n = 1.7 ± 0.2; under the same lifetime extrapolation this gives approximately 25–45 kyr. The spread is driven by how recovery and persistent shifts are separated from secular evolution, not merely by measurement noise. [Akbal et al. 2017, §5](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stx1095) · [Espinoza et al. 2017](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/publications/new-long-term-braking-index-measurements-for-glitching-pulsars-us/) · [P20](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15004)
- **Newer timing check.** Grover et al. (2026), using 2016–2025 timing and extrapolated pre-glitch rates, obtain n = 2.94 ± 0.55. Inserting its central value in 2 tau_c/(n−1) gives about 11.6 kyr (our conversion, not a separately measured age). Their estimate assumes almost complete recovery; it reinforces the dependence on the treatment of glitches. [Grover et al., §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02100v2)
- **Lifetime extrapolation and birth period.** For constant n>1 and torque coefficient, t = [2 tau_c/(n−1)] [1−(P0/P)^(n−1)]. A non-negligible P0 reduces the age relative to the P0 << P estimate. Neither a measured short-baseline braking index nor a recovery model establishes that the same law operated throughout the star's life. The adopted 40 kyr ceiling is a rounded review choice, not the exact 45 kyr endpoint of the low-n extrapolation. The 11 kyr floor likewise is a selection, not a model-independent lower bound.

### Luminosity decision · 18 November 2026

**Adopt L∞ = (2.0–4.4) × 10³² erg/s, together with the adopted age range 11–40 kyr.** V13 is excluded from Vela's adopted age and luminosity envelope; its entries remain in the historical comparison only. These rounded bounds are a review selection across the accepted interpretations and their quoted errors, without a common statistical confidence level. They are not a new fit and do not claim to encompass every possible mass, radius, distance or spectral model.

- **Cool versus total thermal emission.** Manzali et al. (2007), Table 6, give Lcool = 2.3 (+0.5/−0.4) × 10³² and Lhot = 8.3 (+2.2/−1.8) × 10³¹ erg/s; the total is 3.1 (+0.5/−0.4) × 10³² at 287 pc. The hotter component, with apparent radius about 0.73 km, may arise in a polar region. The cooler component has T∞ ≈ 1.06 MK and apparent radius about 5.1 km. The cool-component versus total-thermal distinction changes what is being compared with passive cooling. The selected lower bound 2.0 × 10³² is rounded and is not the exact 1.9 × 10³² endpoint of the quoted cool-BB interval. [Manzali et al. 2007, Table 6 and discussion](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.3194)
- **Atmosphere and emitting-area assumptions.** P20 describes a canonical hydrogen-atmosphere plus power-law solution with M=1.4 solar masses, R=10 km, inferred d≈269 pc and L∞ = (2.61 ± 0.06) × 10³². Its preferred Ofengeim & Zyuzin atmosphere treatment, with fixed M=1.4 solar masses, R=13 km and d=287 pc, gives T∞=0.700 ± 0.005 MK and L∞=(4.24 ± 0.12) × 10³². The atmosphere has a nonuniform surface distribution associated with a dipole field. The approximately 60% difference in luminosity is much larger than the narrow statistical errors within either fixed model. Stellar radius, redshift, temperature distribution and spectral bolometric correction matter; distance differences alone do not explain the spread. [P20, object 21](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15004) · [Ofengeim & Zyuzin 2018](https://doi.org/10.3390/particles1010014)
- **Other systematic effects retained in the discussion.** Parallax constrains the distance near 280–290 pc, but fixed-distance fit errors do not propagate its uncertainty automatically. Interstellar absorption, separation of the pulsar from its bright wind nebula, spectral decomposition and phase dependence also affect the inferred thermal output. Mass–radius variations can enlarge the model dependence beyond the single fit used for the upper endpoint. The selected 2.0–4.4 band is accepted for this catalogue stage without claiming an exhaustive recalculation of those effects. [Manzali et al. 2007](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.3194) · [P20](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15004)
- **Plot convention.** The shaded rectangle spans t=11–40 kyr and L∞=(2.0–4.4) × 10³² erg/s. The previous catalogue marker at 22 kyr, L∞=4.24 × 10³² is retained as a reference location; it is not a newly selected best age or luminosity. Its narrow error bars show the previous compilation assumptions. The rectangle does not assign equal probability to its interior or encode age–luminosity covariance.

## 4. Monogem · PSR B0656+14

- **Age.** V13 gives log₁₀(t/yr) ≈ 4.9, or about 79 kyr. Thorsett et al. support the association using parallax and proper motion, and quote an ~86 kyr model age for the expanding remnant at ~300 pc. P20/web leave the independent age blank, with τc about 111 kyr. The association and remnant modelling support an age of order 0.1 Myr. Neither estimate comes with a directly measured statistical error on the remnant age. [Thorsett et al. 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0306462) · [V13, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.2156)
- **Spectrum.** V13 gives (5.0–6.3) × 10³² from 2BB+PL, with the 100 eV and 50 eV entries describing different components. P20 adopts the Arumugasamy et al. 2BB+PL fit with a Gaussian absorption line and L∞ = 6.7 (+2.1/−1.5) × 10³². The web update uses Zharikov et al.’s preferred broad-band fit and gives (3.2–3.8) × 10³². Zharikov obtains ~68 eV for the cooler component and an emitting area consistent with a nonuniform surface. The distance is close to 0.28–0.29 kpc in these analyses. The change primarily reflects fitting choices and area rather than a revised distance. The apparent difference in M24 Fig. 1 remains a figure reading without the underlying numbers. [P20, object 20](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15004) · [Zharikov et al. 2021, Table 7 and discussion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.07459) · [Web](https://www.ioffe.ru/astro/NSG/thermal/cooldat.html)
- **Decision for discussion.** Whether to pair a remnant-based age with the later multiwavelength fit, and whether to compare the bulk-surface or total thermal output. The current comparison pairs V13 values because the web row lacks an independent age.

## 5. S147 · PSR J0538+2817

- **Age.** Based on motion relative to the remnant, Kramer et al. estimate about 30 kyr and Ng et al. favour ~40 kyr. P20 uses 40 ± 20 kyr. The web interval is 20–60 kyr, with a 30 kyr central value. τc ≈ 620 kyr. With constant dipole braking and n = 3, a true age of 20–60 kyr implies P₀/P ≈ 0.95–0.98, illustrating the effect of a long birth period. [Kramer et al. 2003](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2003ApJ...593L..31K/abstract) · [Ng et al. 2007](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007ApJ...654..487N/abstract)
- **Spectrum and distance.** V13 gives (5.0–7.9) × 10³² with BB+PL at about 1.3 kpc. P20 gives (6.3–13.6) × 10³² based on the NSA family. Ng et al. measure a parallax distance 1.47 (+0.42/−0.27) kpc. Kochanek et al. obtain a narrower **90%** remnant distance interval of 1.30–1.47 kpc, median 1.37 kpc. The archived web row changes the distance while retaining the P20 luminosity. At fixed flux, going from 1.47 to 1.37 kpc would multiply a luminosity by ~0.87. A decision to rescale needs the exact distance or normalization used in the quoted spectral fit. [P20, object 16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15004) · [Kochanek et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13892) · [Web](https://www.ioffe.ru/astro/NSG/thermal/cooldat.html)

## Review principle adopted on 18 November 2026

Expanded uncertainty bars will be a central result of this review. For each source, the displayed interval will encompass the reported measurement uncertainty and the spread across the plausible spectral, distance, absorption, emitting-area and age assumptions examined in the literature.

For quantity q and alternative analysis or physical scenario j with reported endpoints q_(j,lo) and q_(j,hi), use

    q_lo = min_j q_(j,lo)
    q_hi = max_j q_(j,hi).

Where a component has only a central estimate, that value can extend the envelope; the absence of a published statistical interval is recorded. The extrema have an explicit reference and assumption attached. Reported confidence levels are retained so readers can see what uncertainty entered each alternative. The combined interval describes the range supported by the reviewed alternatives; a confidence level requires an additional probabilistic construction.

The plotted central marker is a documented fiducial value within the envelope. A geometric midpoint may be used for a logarithmic overview plot when no fiducial model is selected. This plotting choice carries no probability weighting.

Age, distance and spectral parameters are propagated together within each scenario before taking the outer bounds. The catalogue retains those scenario combinations because independent horizontal and vertical bars enclose parameter combinations that may be unsupported by any single scenario. Timing ages enter as age scenarios with their braking assumptions documented.

Confirmed numerical or transcription errors are corrected before the envelope is formed. An unresolved convention discrepancy remains an explicitly flagged alternative while it is investigated. Repeated compilations of the same fit share their provenance.

The target for cooling comparisons is the photon luminosity associated with cooling. A hot-spot interpretation, hidden surface contribution and whole-surface atmosphere fit must first be translated into the constraints each places on that target. Upper limits keep their one-sided form.

For B2334+61, the 18 November discussion record in §2 now adopts M24’s distance and restricts the working comparison to P20 and M24. Its provisional luminosity envelope is [2.4 × 10³¹, 2.5 × 10³²] erg/s; a self-consistent age interval at that distance remains unresolved. Earlier nearby-distance and direct McGowan-label branches are retained only as audit history. Each future remnant-age scenario must use compatible distance and dynamical assumptions.
