# Literature audit for neutron-star cooling comparisons

**14 November 2026 · Rotation-powered pulsars and X-ray isolated neutron stars · B2334+61 discussion updated 18 November 2026**

This review covers the 40 original entries in these classes in the project, including the pulsars with upper limits and the hot-spot comparison group. The detailed cases below were checked against the cited papers and the original tables where available. The source register also includes cases reviewed through the published compilation. Cas A is now included as an accepted reference, Vela Jr is now included provisionally as a second CCO exception, bringing the current plotted sample to 41 sources after the exclusions recorded below; other CCOs remain excluded. Catalogue selections remain provisional unless explicitly accepted below.

**Conventions.** Luminosities below are redshifted bolometric luminosities in erg/s unless a band is specified. Spectral colour temperatures and atmosphere effective temperatures refer to their fitted components. Age from proper motion depends on the inferred birthplace. A remnant age may also depend on distance, expansion history or an assumed association. The characteristic age τc = P/(2Ṗ) follows from current timing. The difference between these estimators is generally a physical or model-dependent difference. A failure of tabulated P and Ṗ to reproduce the tabulated τc is a numerical inconsistency. At fixed spectral model and unabsorbed bolometric flux, L∞ scales as d².

**Reference keys.** [V13 — Viganò et al. 2013, Tables 1 and 3](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stt1008) · [P20 — Potekhin et al. 2020, Tables 1 and 2 and §3.3](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/staa1871) · [Web — Potekhin compilation, 3 August 2026 snapshot](https://www.ioffe.ru/astro/NSG/thermal/cooldat.html) · [M24 — Marino et al. 2024, Extended Data Tables 1–2 and Supplementary Data Table 6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-024-02291-y). The web table often inherits the P20 spectral analysis or adopts a named later analysis. Its rows and P20 therefore often represent the same evidence.

## B1951+32 · age, distance and whole-surface limit adopted · 18 November 2026

**Source:** PSR B1951+32 / J1952+3252 in CTB 80 (source 30). **Adopt age 45–85 kyr**, as accepted by the user. This rounded review envelope covers the favoured kinematic and remnant estimates, not a common statistical confidence interval. The characteristic age 107 kyr remains documented outside the adopted interval. Retain the previous 64 kyr plotting marker, not a new best estimate. Distance 1–2 kpc and the whole-surface luminosity upper limit are now adopted below; imported values remain as historical records.

### Age evidence and causes of discrepancy

- [Migliazzo et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1086/340002), **Migliazzo2002**: 64 ± 18 kyr from proper motion. They allow an offset birthplace using a geometric probability prescription; the result is not solely a statistical proper-motion error and does not require an exactly central explosion. It still assumes an approximate remnant centre and their birthplace geometry prior. The angular traceback is independent of the assumed distance in their construction.
- [Zeiger et al. 2008, section 4.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0801.0244), **Zeiger2008**: improved proper motion gives approximately 51 kyr for closest approach to a geometric centre fitted to the shell. Uncertain explosion position and asymmetric expansion dominate the interpretation. Their path misses the earlier H I expansion centre; the approximately 77 kyr Koo et al. (1990) reconstruction is therefore not independently confirmed by the pulsar trajectory.
- [Leahy & Ranasinghe 2012](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2966.2012.20909.x), **LeahyRanasinghe2012**: a decelerating outer H I shell interpretation yields an age around 60 kyr, consistent with the adopted interval. This is a dynamical model, not an exact clock.
- Characteristic age is approximately 107 kyr. For constant n=3 braking, t=tau_c[1-(P0/P)^2]; the present 39.5 ms period and a birth period approximately 25–29 ms can explain the younger 51–64 kyr estimates. The birth period is inferred conditionally, not measured. Migliazzo et al. quote 27 ± 6 ms allowing a range of braking indices. Changing the birth period or braking history changes the mapping from characteristic to true age.

### Distance evidence · no new distance adopted

The spectral analysis by [Li, Lu & Li 2005](https://doi.org/10.1086/430941), **Li2005**, assumes **2.0 kpc**. The P20 luminosity discussion also normalizes to 2.0 kpc, whereas its distance table carries **2.4 ± 0.2 kpc**, the older dispersion-measure estimate also cited by Migliazzo et al. A table distance must not silently be substituted into a luminosity normalized elsewhere.

**LeahyRanasinghe2012** reanalyse H I absorption toward CTB 80. The highest absorption velocity gives near/far alternatives of 2.1/4.0 kpc before allowing gas velocity dispersion; allowing dispersion changes these to approximately 1.1/5.0 kpc. They favour the near branch using pulsar dispersion-measure evidence, and adopt a nominal **1.5 kpc**. The approximately **1.1–2.1 kpc** near-branch bracket is a kinematic/model range, not a Gaussian measurement. Dependence on Galactic rotation, gas velocities and near/far branch selection must remain explicit. The older 2.4 ± 0.2 kpc estimate depends on the free-electron density model; no direct pulsar parallax has been established by these cited papers.

### Thermal components and luminosity · selection pending

**Li2005**, sections 3.2 and 4.4: absorbed PL+BB improves the fit relative to PL alone. At d=2 kpc, kT=0.13 ± 0.02 keV and equivalent BB radius 2.2 (+1.4/−0.8) km; their ordinary fit errors are 90% intervals. Absorption was fixed to N_H=3.0e21 cm^-2 from the surrounding nebular spectra, and estimated 6% pile-up was neglected. These choices introduce additional systematics. The authors interpret the small emitting area as hot spots; it is not a uniquely measured whole-surface cooling luminosity. Their reported F-test significance is not an independent modern validation of the thermal-component detection.

**Potekhin2020**, object 30, translates this detected component into L_inf=1.8 (+3.0/−1.1)e32 (d/2 kpc)^2 erg/s. This is the existing catalogue marker/range. At fixed fitted flux, nominal values are 1.01e32 at 1.5 kpc, 1.8e32 at 2.0 kpc, and 2.59e32 at 2.4 kpc. The corresponding quoted fit intervals are (0.394–2.70), (0.70–4.80), and (1.008–6.912)e32 erg/s. These are sensitivity calculations, not additional measurements; parameter covariance is not reconstructed from marginal temperature/radius errors.

**Distinct whole-surface constraint:** Li2005 also replace the small-area interpretation by a PL+BB fit with fixed 12 km emitting radius. They obtain a 3-sigma temperature ceiling of 0.78 MK; P20 expresses this as L_inf < 3.8e32 erg/s at the reference distance. The hot-spot component was not subtracted in deriving that conservative ceiling. This is not a fitted atmosphere alternative or a positive lower bound. A distance change at fixed physical radius requires refitting the normalization/temperature; simply multiplying this ceiling by d^2 is only a fixed-flux sensitivity prescription, not a new self-consistent upper limit. P20 adopts redshifted observational conventions; Li's fitted BB radius should not be silently mixed with a separately chosen physical radius/redshift.

### Distance and luminosity decision adopted · 18 November 2026

**Adopt distance 1–2 kpc and the published whole-surface ceiling L_inf < 3.8e32 erg/s**, together with age 45–85 kyr. The selected distance is a rounded review bracket, not a combined confidence interval or an assertion that every older distance estimate lies within it. The older dispersion-measure 2.4 ± 0.2 kpc branch is documented but excluded from the adopted distance bracket.

The plot now replaces the small-area hot-spot detection with a downward upper-limit arrow at 3.8e32 erg/s and horizontal age extent 45–85 kyr. The 64 kyr age anchor is retained for display; there is no positive luminosity lower bound, symmetric luminosity error bar or shaded box. The original comparison figure and imported measurements preserve the detected component.

This ceiling retains the published 3-sigma PL+BB fit with fixed 12 km emitting radius and reference distance 2 kpc, the upper endpoint of the accepted distance bracket. We do not rescale it to a midpoint or claim a new 1-kpc fit. At fixed flux a d-squared scaling would lower a luminosity, but changing distance at fixed physical emitting radius requires a spectral refit; the retained ceiling is a pragmatic reference constraint, not a demonstrated uniform-confidence bound across all distances or atmosphere models. Earlier discussion of pending selections above is superseded by this decision.


## IC 443 · age discussion opened · 18 November 2026

**Source:** CXOU J061705.3+222127 (J0617; source 17), a neutron-star candidate powering a PWN. **Adopt age 3–20 kyr.** Exclude the old Chevalier estimate from the selected envelope; retain it only as historical provenance. Exclude NS cooling-model age arguments from the age evidence. The old 30 kyr marker lies outside the adopted interval, so the current plot uses the geometric midpoint 7.75 kyr as a display anchor, not a best estimate. Luminosity range adopted below. A measured spin-down age is unavailable: estimated period/field values inferred from PWN correlations must not be treated as timing measurements.

### Independent age scenarios and causes

- [Chevalier 1999](https://doi.org/10.1086/306710), key **Chevalier1999**: approximately 30 kyr from a radiative-shell model in a clumpy molecular environment, using t≈0.3R/v, R≈7.4 pc and shock speed ≈100 km/s. This is a remnant-dynamics argument independent of NS cooling, not a precise age measurement. The current 25–35 kyr display interval describes this older scenario rather than encompassing the literature spread.
- [Troja et al. 2008, §4.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.1049), key **Troja2008**: about 4 kyr from the forward/reverse-shock radii in a uniform low-density medium, and about 3 kyr in a wind simulation. They discuss underestimation if the forward shock has been strongly decelerated by cavity walls, and favour an age below 10 kyr. Shock identification, density profile and cloud impact cause the difference from the older radiative model.
- [Ustamujic et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202039940), key **Ustamujic2021**: approximately 8 kyr, with a preferred state around 8.4 kyr, from 3D hydrodynamics reproducing the complex X-ray morphology in an inhomogeneous cloud environment and an off-centre explosion near the reconstructed birthplace of J0617. This is a model-selected evolutionary stage, not a small statistical age error.
- [Dinçel et al. 2026, §5.2](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202556086), key **Dincel2026**: favour 10–20 kyr under their proposed HD 254577 former-binary-companion association and typical NS velocities. They examine assumed ages and predicted NS velocities; this is not an independently measured common flight-time solution. The companion/explosion-site identification and velocity prior are explicit assumptions.

### Association, geometry and circularity

[Swartz et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-637X/808/1/84), key **Swartz2015**, report only a proper-motion upper limit of 44 mas/yr at 99% confidence. Birth at the apparent remnant centre then suggests age ≳20 kyr. This geometric constraint changes for an off-centre explosion. PWN orientation is not a measured velocity vector. Their comparison with cooling models favouring a few tens of kyr must not be reused as an independent age constraint in a cooling test.

**Age decision adopted 18 November 2026:** 3–20 kyr, selected from the younger remnant-dynamics and recent association scenarios above. No common confidence level; this supersedes the earlier proposed 3–35 kyr envelope. The age will not be selected using cooling curves. Distance and luminosity decisions are recorded below.

### Luminosity robustness · discussion, no adopted expansion

[Swartz et al. 2015, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.05507): NSA gives L∞=(2.6±0.1) × 10³² erg/s; BB gives 1.0 (+0.2/−0.1) × 10³². Both fit acceptably. NSA fixes d=1.5 kpc, M=1.4 M☉, R=10 km and B=10¹³ G; BB has R∞≈1.63 km. Quoted errors are 1σ. Thus the catalogue's narrow NSA band excludes model dependence; the smaller BB component need not trace whole-surface cooling.

**Review calculation:** at the published distance, the two quoted fit intervals span (0.9–2.7) × 10³². A fixed-bolometric-flux d² sensitivity using the imported 1.4–2.0 kpc interval gives endpoints 0.78–4.8 × 10³², rounded ≈(0.8–5) × 10³². This is a conditional diagnostic, not a re-fit: changing distance in a fixed-radius atmosphere model changes its normalization and can alter fitted temperature, absorption and bolometric correction. This earlier diagnostic is superseded by the adopted range below. The existing 1.7 kpc catalogue distance must not be mistaken for the fixed 1.5 kpc used in that spectral analysis.

### Distance adopted and luminosity sensitivity · 18 November 2026

**Adopt 1.6–1.7 kpc as the working distance range**, with 1.65 kpc only a convenient midpoint. This is a user-selected review interval, not a combined statistical confidence interval; the environmental/association assumptions of the supporting studies can overlap.

- [Yu et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stz1940), **Yu2019**: 1.729 (+0.116/−0.094) kpc from 3D dust mapping and cloud/remnant morphology.
- [Dinçel et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stae1307), **Dincel2024**: 1.6 ± 0.15 kpc from stellar extinction and absorption constraints on the remnant environment.
- [Dinçel et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202556086), **Dincel2026**: Gaia-based HD 254577 distance 1.701 (+0.055/−0.054) kpc, transferred through their proposed former-companion association. The NS distance is not itself a parallax measurement.

**Conditional calculation, keeping fitted bolometric flux fixed:** relative to the spectral paper's 1.5 kpc, factors (1.6/1.5)²=1.138 and (1.7/1.5)²=1.284 increase luminosities by 14–28%. The spread across the adopted distance interval alone is (1.7/1.6)²=1.129, about 13% peak-to-peak (approximately ±6% around its midpoint).

NSA central luminosity would shift from 2.6 to 2.96–3.34 × 10³² erg/s; BB from 1.0 to 1.14–1.28 × 10³². Applying the same scaling to the quoted fit endpoints gives NSA 2.84–3.47 and BB 1.02–1.54, all in units 10³² erg/s. Their combined conditional envelope is about (1.0–3.5) × 10³², narrower than the earlier diagnostic using 1.4–2.0 kpc. It is not a new fit or a common-confidence interval. In particular, the fixed-radius NSA normalization would require a spectral refit for a self-consistent new-distance result. The adopted bounds below supersede this pending proposal; the previous luminosity marker is retained for comparison.

## 1. PSR J0205+6449 · 3C 58

- **Age provenance.** P20 uses 819 yr at the spectral epoch and M24 gives 841 yr in its main text. Both rely on the association with SN 1181. Kothes supports that association along with a distance of 2.0 ± 0.3 kpc. Ritter et al. identify Pa 30 with SN 1181. Radio expansion of 3C 58 gives a much longer constant-expansion timescale, roughly 7 kyr, which is sensitive to the expansion history. With the P and Ṗ in M24 Extended Data Table 1, τc = **5.38 kyr**, versus 58.8 kyr in that table and 54 kyr in the Methods. The spin-down value and the radio expansion timescale have different physical assumptions. [Kothes 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.8384) · [Ritter et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/ac2253) · [Bietenholz 2006](https://doi.org/10.1086/504584) · [M24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05371)
- **Thermal alternatives at 2.0 kpc.** M24 finds a BB+PL fit with kT∞ = 200 (+80/−40) eV, nominal BB radius 0.6 km and hot-spot L∞ = 1.44 (+0.60/−0.50) × 10³². Its additional 11 km blackbody gives Lcool < 10²⁸ under that fit. P20 reanalyses Chandra with NSMAXG+PL and fixes M = 1.4 M☉ and R = 13 km. It gives a whole-surface kT∞ near 49 eV and L∞ = 1.9 (+1.5/−1.1) × 10³². The detected components have overlapping bolometric luminosities and very different temperatures and emitting areas. The M24 limit tests an extra cool blackbody conditional on the hot-spot model. [M24, Methods and tables](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05371) · [P20, object 14](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15004)
- **Decision status.** Age 0.8–7 kyr is adopted provisionally below, with a young decelerating-expansion scenario and several-kyr alternatives retained; association with SN 1181 is not assumed. The luminosity envelope below is adopted; the spectral interpretation remains a caveat.

### Age evidence and retained review envelope · 19 November 2026

**Retain the shaded box at age 0.8–7 kyr and L∞ = (0.2–3.4) × 10³² erg/s.** It is a selected envelope spanning methodological and interpretive alternatives, not a statistical confidence region, a uniform probability distribution, or a demonstration that every combination of age and luminosity is supported by an individual analysis. The endpoints do not have equal evidential weight. The age remains an open issue; no change is made to the luminosity selection.

- **Optical ejecta:** [Fesen et al. (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1086/522781) identify distinct fast and slow knot populations and measure approximate proper motions 0.02–0.07 arcsec/yr, below the approximately 0.2 arcsec/yr expected for free expansion since 1181. The frequently cited age of about 3 kyr is an expansion interpretation, not a model-independent age measurement. Knot identification, geometry, explosion centre and deceleration matter; different filaments need not share a single velocity history.
- **Radio PWN expansion:** [Bietenholz (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1086/504584) measures 0.014 ± 0.003 percent/yr, implying an inverse fractional expansion rate of approximately 7 kyr. This angular timescale does not require a distance, unlike the inferred physical velocity. Radio synchrotron structures trace the PWN rather than necessarily freely expanding supernova ejecta; changing expansion, deceleration or acceleration, pulsar energy injection, and interaction with ejecta affect the relation to true age. The rounded 7 kyr endpoint is an editorial choice, not a firm upper bound or the full uncertainty range.
- **Young dynamical alternative:** [Kothes (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201219839) argues that deceleration can make free-expansion ages overestimate the actual age and obtains an approximately 0.63–1.6 kyr model-dependent range. Its assumptions about the knot population, spherical dynamics and evolutionary phase are not uniquely established. The retained 0.8 kyr endpoint is a rounded continuity choice within the young branch, not the minimum age allowed by Kothes. Retention of this branch does not require identifying 3C 58 with the 1181 event.
- **SN 1181 and Pa 30:** The historical date is no longer used as an established age for 3C 58. [Ritter et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/ac2253) identify Pa 30 as the likely remnant; [Cunningham et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/ad713b) find near-ballistic ejecta with an inferred explosion epoch 1152 (+77/−75) CE, consistent with 1181. This makes the older association with 3C 58 considerably less compelling, but Pa 30's association alone does not exclude a different young explosion of 3C 58. The earlier historical reasoning below and in imported catalogues remains provenance, not the justification for the retained young endpoint.
- **Timing and pulsar motion:** P and Pdot imply characteristic age approximately 5.4 kyr; its relation to elapsed time depends on birth period and torque history, so it is neither a direct measurement nor a universal upper limit. The 54 and 58.8 kyr values printed in M24 are inconsistent with its listed timing parameters and are not alternate physical ages. The measured pulsar proper motion does not itself select a particular kinematic age, as explicitly stated by [Bietenholz et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stt353).
- **Selection and circularity:** The shaded interval is a pragmatic representation of the reviewed scenarios, not a rigorous bound covering all possible models. Its lower endpoint is not asserted to be a measured age. Do not select the age using neutron-star cooling curves, and keep the evidence for each age and spectral scenario available separately.

### Age decision and causes of the spread · 18 November 2026

**Adopted working age envelope: 11–40 kyr.** This is the review's selected range encompassing the main younger and older age scenarios. It has no statistical confidence level and is not a rigorous bound on all published models. It deliberately replaces V13's 5–16 kyr bracket and the narrower P20 compilation intervals for the current review. No central age has been selected here. The luminosity decision is recorded below.

- **Compilation versus independent evidence.** V13 Table 1 reports log10(t/yr) = 3.7–4.2, or about 5–16 kyr, labelled SNR. Its 5 kyr endpoint is not retained in the adopted range. P20 Table 2 gives 17–23 kyr, whereas its object discussion selects 17–27 kyr. This is an internal mismatch, not two independent age measurements. The discrepancy is retained for provenance. [V13, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.2156) · [P20, §3.3 object 21 and Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15004)
- **Remnant morphology and ejecta dynamics.** P20 summarizes Aschenbach et al. (1995) as 18 ± 9 kyr from extended X-ray features and notes an independent approximately 18 kyr estimate by Jenkins, Silk & Wallerstein (1976). Aschenbach (2002) compares the angular offset of silicon-rich fragment A, about 5.3 degrees from the inferred explosion centre, with velocities expected from progenitor/explosion models. At 250 pc and 11.4 kyr the implied mean speed is about 2000 km/s, higher than the relevant model silicon velocities. This favours an older remnant. The source describes the age as at least roughly 17–23 kyr, rather than measuring a bounded interval with 23 kyr as an upper limit. Distance, explosion centre, progenitor structure, ejecta mixing and deceleration are systematic assumptions. A direct expansion clock was not established by that argument. [Aschenbach 2002, §2.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0208492) · [P20](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15004)
- **Characteristic age.** tau_c = P/(2 Pdot) ≈ 11.3 kyr is a timing estimator assuming constant dipole braking (n=3), a constant torque coefficient and a negligible birth period. It is not a direct determination of elapsed time.
- **Glitch-recovery treatment.** Akbal et al. (2017) extrapolate timing fits to rates just before successive glitches and infer n = 2.81 ± 0.12, giving about 12.5 kyr if extrapolated as a constant braking law with negligible birth period. This is not simply an instantaneous pre-glitch index. Espinoza, Lyne & Stappers (2017) use a different treatment of cumulative glitch effects and obtain n = 1.7 ± 0.2; under the same lifetime extrapolation this gives approximately 25–45 kyr. The spread is driven by how recovery and persistent shifts are separated from secular evolution, not merely by measurement noise. [Akbal et al. 2017, §5](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stx1095) · [Espinoza et al. 2017](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/publications/new-long-term-braking-index-measurements-for-glitching-pulsars-us/) · [P20](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15004)
- **Newer timing check.** Grover et al. (2026), using 2016–2025 timing and extrapolated pre-glitch rates, obtain n = 2.94 ± 0.55. Inserting its central value in 2 tau_c/(n−1) gives about 11.6 kyr (our conversion, not a separately measured age). Their estimate assumes almost complete recovery; it reinforces the dependence on the treatment of glitches. [Grover et al., §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02100v2)
- **Lifetime extrapolation and birth period.** For constant n>1 and torque coefficient, t = [2 tau_c/(n−1)] [1−(P0/P)^(n−1)]. A non-negligible P0 reduces the age relative to the P0 << P estimate. Neither a measured short-baseline braking index nor a recovery model establishes that the same law operated throughout the star's life. The adopted 40 kyr ceiling is a rounded review choice, not the exact 45 kyr endpoint of the low-n extrapolation. The 11 kyr floor likewise is a selection, not a model-independent lower bound.

## Review principle adopted on 18 November 2026

Expanded uncertainty bars will be a central result of this review. For each source, the displayed interval will encompass the reported measurement uncertainty and the spread across the plausible spectral, distance, absorption, emitting-area and age assumptions examined in the literature.

For quantity q and alternative analysis or physical scenario j with reported endpoints q_(j,lo) and q_(j,hi), use

    q_lo = min_j q_(j,lo)
    q_hi = max_j q_(j,hi).

Where a component has only a central estimate, that value can extend the envelope; the absence of a published statistical interval is recorded. The extrema have an explicit reference and assumption attached. Reported confidence levels are retained so readers can see what uncertainty entered each alternative. The combined interval describes the range supported by the reviewed alternatives; a confidence level requires an additional probabilistic construction.

The plotted central marker is a documented fiducial value within the envelope. A geometric midpoint may be used for a logarithmic overview plot when no fiducial model is selected. This plotting choice carries no probability weighting.

Age, distance and spectral parameters are propagated together within each scenario before taking the outer bounds. The catalogue retains those scenario combinations because independent horizontal and vertical bars enclose parameter combinations that may be unsupported by any single scenario. Timing ages enter as age scenarios with their braking assumptions documented.

Confirmed numerical or transcription errors are corrected before the envelope is formed. An unresolved convention discrepancy remains an explicitly flagged alternative while it is investigated. Repeated compilations of the same fit share their provenance.

The target for cooling comparisons is the photon luminosity associated with cooling. A hot-spot interpretation, hidden surface contribution and whole-surface atmosphere fit must first be translated into the constraints each places on that target. Upper limits keep their one-sided form.

For B2334+61, the 18 November discussion record in §2 now adopts M24’s distance and restricts the working comparison to P20 and M24. Its provisional luminosity envelope is [2.4 × 10³¹, 2.5 × 10³²] erg/s; a self-consistent age interval at that distance remains unresolved.
