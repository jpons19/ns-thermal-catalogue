How to read the values and colours

Each source has separate age, distance, flux, temperature and magnetic-field assessments. Agreed means agreement within the stated comparison or an accepted reference interpretation; shared assumptions are identified in the explanation. Adopted / revised marks a selected review range or published revision, without implying consensus. Open issue marks unresolved alternatives or conventions. Disputed marks a contested association or identity. Limited means insufficient independent evidence, missing checks, a conditional component or an upper limit.

Flux assesses the thermal spectral component and its bolometric correction, not distance alone. A luminosity change caused only by d² belongs in the distance column; the measurement tables continue to report L∞. Bolometric fluxes have not been newly fitted. A BB versus atmosphere temperature difference can affect both flux and temperature. Adopted uncertainty envelopes can therefore coexist with open spectral issues.

Hover over a cell for its reason, or expand the source to read all five explanations. Grey does not mean agreement. These indicators summarize the existing audit; sources outside the review scope remain limited unless explicitly assessed. Physical class and eligibility for cooling remain separate. Temperatures retain the model, component and redshift conventions of each reference.

Numbers inside the coloured cells: accepted review ranges take precedence; otherwise the named catalogue fit is shown. Age marked τc is a spin-down proxy. Flux is a derived diagnostic in 10⁻¹³ erg cm⁻² s⁻¹ at one stated reference distance; the accompanying L∞ range is in 10³² erg/s. It is not a new flux measurement or a common-confidence interval. Temperature remains fit/component dependent. Bdip is an equatorial canonical dipole estimate in 10¹² G; V13 polar estimates are divided by two. Missing fields remain blank (—). Vela Jr · included for discussion · 18 November 2026

Include CXOU J085201.4−461753 (source 3) as a second CCO exception. Age 1.6–5.1 kyr and Ho2026 cool-surface luminosity (1.3–4.0) × 10³² erg/s adopted; distance and spectral assumptions remain caveats. The adopted open green star, labelled simply Vela Jr. in the plot to reduce crowding, uses the Ho2026 cool-surface reference: adopted age 1.6–5.1 kyr, L∞=(1.3–4.0) × 10³² erg/s. Its geometric display location is not a best estimate. Age and luminosity ranges are shown as error bars without a shaded box. Imported WEB luminosities mix cool and total emission and are not used as a single physical-component interval here.

Age scenarios for the next decision

  • Allen et al. 2015, Allen2015: 2.4–5.1 kyr from NW-rim Chandra expansion plus hydrodynamics in a uniform medium. Their measured 0.42 ± 0.10 arcsec/yr differs from the earlier XMM value 0.84 ± 0.23. They allow ages up to about 50% larger for expansion into a steady wind: an approximate upper scenario near 7.7 kyr, not a new confidence limit.
  • Marino et al. 2024, Marino2024: adopted 2.5–5 kyr; retain as the imported analysis choice rather than an independent age measurement.
  • Suherli et al. 2026, preprint 2025, Suherli2026: approximately 1.6–3.3 kyr using Sedov-Taylor evolution and the two expansion measurements. The quoted range reflects different rates, not a statistical confidence interval. Their revised distance is 1.41 ± 0.14 kpc, transferred from Ve 7–27 through a proposed shared physical environment; it is not a parallax of the neutron star.

Review inference: t=m R/v=m θ/μ. For the same angular radius, proper motion and assumed deceleration parameter m, distance cancels. Thus the newer, younger age branch must not be attributed simply to the larger distance: expansion-rate selection and dynamical assumptions cause it. The user adopts 1.6–5.1 kyr, spanning the principal uniform-medium/Sedov branches. Allen's older steady-wind scenario remains a caveat outside these selected bounds, which have no common confidence level.

Thermal selection and model caveats

Ho, Simkhayeva & Potekhin 2026, Ho2026, analyse all available XMM/Chandra spectra. Their hydrogen-atmosphere interpretation separates the cool surface, L∞=(1.3–4.0) × 10³², from total luminosity (5 ± 1) × 10³² dominated by a hot spot, at d≈1.4 kpc. The cool range spans model medians, rather than enclosing every fit's statistical and distance uncertainty. They retain Allen's 2.4–5.1 kyr age for their cooling diagram. This is the selected cool-surface plotting reference.

Imported earlier alternatives remain visible: P20 whole-surface luminosity (1–3) × 10³³ at d≈0.77 kpc; M24 small-area BB luminosity (1.4–2.6) × 10³² and a separate additional-cool-surface upper limit 1.3 × 10³². These are different emitting components and models, not repeated measurements of one agreed cooling luminosity. The M24 additional-surface limit must not be used as a detected lower endpoint. Distance-only rescaling does not replace refitting alternative atmosphere models. The adopted selection and its limitations are recorded below.

Decision · 19 November 2026. Adopt the Ho2026 cool-surface model range L∞=(1.3–4.0) × 10³² erg/s together with the previously adopted age 1.6–5.1 kyr. Display the two ranges as error bars around the geometric display marker (age √(1.6×5.1) ≈ 2.86 kyr; the former 3.50 kyr marker was the geometric midpoint of Allen’s 2.4–5.1 kyr range), without a shaded box. The Ho luminosity endpoints span medians of different spectral models at 1.4 kpc, not a single statistical confidence interval or the full distance and spectral uncertainty. The marker is not a fitted best estimate. The P20 high-luminosity carbon branch is excluded from the adopted selection and retained in the literature table as provenance.

Marino et al. (2024) and Ho et al. (2026) report luminosities compatible in order of magnitude, despite different distances and spectral models. This does not equate the detected small-area blackbody luminosity in Marino with Ho's cool-surface component: Marino's additional whole-surface blackbody is a conditional upper limit, not a detection or a lower error-bar endpoint. Marino analyses Chandra ACIS-S; Ho uses that observation plus XMM spectra, so analyses are independent but the observations partially overlap. Ho discusses potentially higher ACIS-S flux due to remnant contamination. Distance association, stellar radius, composition, absorption and component decomposition remain systematic caveats.

Decision · 20 November 2026 (closes the Vela Jr discussion)

This complements the 19 November decision above, which already adopts the Ho2026 luminosity and the 2.86 kyr marker.

  • Distance adopted: 1.41 (1.27–1.55) kpc from Suherli et al. (2026). It is consistent with the Ho2026 analysis, which uses d ≈ 1.4 kpc. The P20 whole-surface value at 0.77 kpc and the M24 small-area value stay as provenance; rescaling them by distance alone is not valid.
  • Why Ho2026. It is the only analysis that uses all available XMM-Newton and Chandra spectra, and it separates the cool surface from the hot spot explicitly.
  • Cooling comparison (report only, not used to select the age). Against the minimal-cooling band of the 2026-11-20 sweeps, the upper end of the luminosity range lies 0.17 dex below the band at 5.1 kyr and 0.43 dex below at 1.6 kyr. It is marginally below, with the tension growing for younger ages, as for J0205.

Age evidence and retained review envelope · 19 November 2026

Retain the shaded box at age 0.8–7 kyr and L∞ = (0.2–3.4) × 10³² erg/s. It is a selected envelope spanning methodological and interpretive alternatives, not a statistical confidence region, a uniform probability distribution, or a demonstration that every combination of age and luminosity is supported by an individual analysis. The endpoints do not have equal evidential weight. The age remains an open issue; no change is made to the luminosity selection.

  • Optical ejecta: Fesen et al. (2008) identify distinct fast and slow knot populations and measure approximate proper motions 0.02–0.07 arcsec/yr, below the approximately 0.2 arcsec/yr expected for free expansion since 1181. The frequently cited age of about 3 kyr is an expansion interpretation, not a model-independent age measurement. Knot identification, geometry, explosion centre and deceleration matter; different filaments need not share a single velocity history.
  • Radio PWN expansion: Bietenholz (2006) measures 0.014 ± 0.003 percent/yr, implying an inverse fractional expansion rate of approximately 7 kyr. This angular timescale does not require a distance, unlike the inferred physical velocity. Radio synchrotron structures trace the PWN rather than necessarily freely expanding supernova ejecta; changing expansion, deceleration or acceleration, pulsar energy injection, and interaction with ejecta affect the relation to true age. The rounded 7 kyr endpoint is an editorial choice, not a firm upper bound or the full uncertainty range.
  • Young dynamical alternative: Kothes (2013) argues that deceleration can make free-expansion ages overestimate the actual age and obtains an approximately 0.63–1.6 kyr model-dependent range. Its assumptions about the knot population, spherical dynamics and evolutionary phase are not uniquely established. The retained 0.8 kyr endpoint is a rounded continuity choice within the young branch, not the minimum age allowed by Kothes. Retention of this branch does not require identifying 3C 58 with the 1181 event.
  • SN 1181 and Pa 30: The historical date is no longer used as an established age for 3C 58. Ritter et al. (2021) identify Pa 30 as the likely remnant; Cunningham et al. (2024) find near-ballistic ejecta with an inferred explosion epoch 1152 (+77/−75) CE, consistent with 1181. This makes the older association with 3C 58 considerably less compelling, but Pa 30's association alone does not exclude a different young explosion of 3C 58. The earlier historical reasoning below and in imported catalogues remains provenance, not the justification for the retained young endpoint.
  • Timing and pulsar motion: P and Pdot imply characteristic age approximately 5.4 kyr; its relation to elapsed time depends on birth period and torque history, so it is neither a direct measurement nor a universal upper limit. The 54 and 58.8 kyr values printed in M24 are inconsistent with its listed timing parameters and are not alternate physical ages. The measured pulsar proper motion does not itself select a particular kinematic age, as explicitly stated by Bietenholz et al. (2013).
  • Selection and circularity: The shaded interval is a pragmatic representation of the reviewed scenarios, not a rigorous bound covering all possible models. Its lower endpoint is not asserted to be a measured age. Do not select the age using neutron-star cooling curves, and keep the evidence for each age and spectral scenario available separately.

Age decision and causes of the spread · 18 November 2026

Adopted working age envelope: 11–40 kyr. This is the review's selected range encompassing the main younger and older age scenarios. It has no statistical confidence level and is not a rigorous bound on all published models. It deliberately replaces V13's 5–16 kyr bracket and the narrower P20 compilation intervals for the current review. No central age has been selected here. The luminosity decision is recorded below.

  • Compilation versus independent evidence. V13 Table 1 reports log10(t/yr) = 3.7–4.2, or about 5–16 kyr, labelled SNR. Its 5 kyr endpoint is not retained in the adopted range. P20 Table 2 gives 17–23 kyr, whereas its object discussion selects 17–27 kyr. This is an internal mismatch, not two independent age measurements. The discrepancy is retained for provenance. V13, Table 1 · P20, §3.3 object 21 and Table 2
  • Remnant morphology and ejecta dynamics. P20 summarizes Aschenbach et al. (1995) as 18 ± 9 kyr from extended X-ray features and notes an independent approximately 18 kyr estimate by Jenkins, Silk & Wallerstein (1976). Aschenbach (2002) compares the angular offset of silicon-rich fragment A, about 5.3 degrees from the inferred explosion centre, with velocities expected from progenitor/explosion models. At 250 pc and 11.4 kyr the implied mean speed is about 2000 km/s, higher than the relevant model silicon velocities. This favours an older remnant. The source describes the age as at least roughly 17–23 kyr, rather than measuring a bounded interval with 23 kyr as an upper limit. Distance, explosion centre, progenitor structure, ejecta mixing and deceleration are systematic assumptions. A direct expansion clock was not established by that argument. Aschenbach 2002, §2.1 · P20
  • Characteristic age. tau_c = P/(2 Pdot) ≈ 11.3 kyr is a timing estimator assuming constant dipole braking (n=3), a constant torque coefficient and a negligible birth period. It is not a direct determination of elapsed time.
  • Glitch-recovery treatment. Akbal et al. (2017) extrapolate timing fits to rates just before successive glitches and infer n = 2.81 ± 0.12, giving about 12.5 kyr if extrapolated as a constant braking law with negligible birth period. This is not simply an instantaneous pre-glitch index. Espinoza, Lyne & Stappers (2017) use a different treatment of cumulative glitch effects and obtain n = 1.7 ± 0.2; under the same lifetime extrapolation this gives approximately 25–45 kyr. The spread is driven by how recovery and persistent shifts are separated from secular evolution, not merely by measurement noise. Akbal et al. 2017, §5 · Espinoza et al. 2017 · P20
  • Newer timing check. Grover et al. (2026), using 2016–2025 timing and extrapolated pre-glitch rates, obtain n = 2.94 ± 0.55. Inserting its central value in 2 tau_c/(n−1) gives about 11.6 kyr (our conversion, not a separately measured age). Their estimate assumes almost complete recovery; it reinforces the dependence on the treatment of glitches. Grover et al., §4.3
  • Lifetime extrapolation and birth period. For constant n>1 and torque coefficient, t = [2 tau_c/(n−1)] [1−(P0/P)^(n−1)]. A non-negligible P0 reduces the age relative to the P0 << P estimate. Neither a measured short-baseline braking index nor a recovery model establishes that the same law operated throughout the star's life. The adopted 40 kyr ceiling is a rounded review choice, not the exact 45 kyr endpoint of the low-n extrapolation. The 11 kyr floor likewise is a selection, not a model-independent lower bound.

Age evidence and causes of discrepancy

  • Migliazzo et al. 2002, Migliazzo2002: 64 ± 18 kyr from proper motion. They allow an offset birthplace using a geometric probability prescription; the result is not solely a statistical proper-motion error and does not require an exactly central explosion. It still assumes an approximate remnant centre and their birthplace geometry prior. The angular traceback is independent of the assumed distance in their construction.
  • Zeiger et al. 2008, section 4.2, Zeiger2008: improved proper motion gives approximately 51 kyr for closest approach to a geometric centre fitted to the shell. Uncertain explosion position and asymmetric expansion dominate the interpretation. Their path misses the earlier H I expansion centre; the approximately 77 kyr Koo et al. (1990) reconstruction is therefore not independently confirmed by the pulsar trajectory.
  • Leahy & Ranasinghe 2012, LeahyRanasinghe2012: a decelerating outer H I shell interpretation yields an age around 60 kyr, consistent with the adopted interval. This is a dynamical model, not an exact clock.
  • Characteristic age is approximately 107 kyr. For constant n=3 braking, t=tau_c[1-(P0/P)^2]; the present 39.5 ms period and a birth period approximately 25–29 ms can explain the younger 51–64 kyr estimates. The birth period is inferred conditionally, not measured. Migliazzo et al. quote 27 ± 6 ms allowing a range of braking indices. Changing the birth period or braking history changes the mapping from characteristic to true age.

Distance evidence · no new distance adopted

The spectral analysis by Li, Lu & Li 2005, Li2005, assumes 2.0 kpc. The P20 luminosity discussion also normalizes to 2.0 kpc, whereas its distance table carries 2.4 ± 0.2 kpc, the older dispersion-measure estimate also cited by Migliazzo et al. A table distance must not silently be substituted into a luminosity normalized elsewhere.

LeahyRanasinghe2012 reanalyse H I absorption toward CTB 80. The highest absorption velocity gives near/far alternatives of 2.1/4.0 kpc before allowing gas velocity dispersion; allowing dispersion changes these to approximately 1.1/5.0 kpc. They favour the near branch using pulsar dispersion-measure evidence, and adopt a nominal 1.5 kpc. The approximately 1.1–2.1 kpc near-branch bracket is a kinematic/model range, not a Gaussian measurement. Dependence on Galactic rotation, gas velocities and near/far branch selection must remain explicit. The older 2.4 ± 0.2 kpc estimate depends on the free-electron density model; no direct pulsar parallax has been established by these cited papers.

Cooling-constraint decision and distance/age caveats · 19 November 2026

Retain the existing orange box: displayed age 6.93–41.1 kyr and L∞=(2.4 × 10³¹–2.5 × 10³²) erg/s. This is a deliberately selected working comparison, not an envelope of every distance/spectral scenario and not a statistical confidence region. The age endpoints remain plotting conventions rather than demonstrated bounds. The user’s aim is to test whether the cold-source tension with a given cooling curve is robust: within the same age and spectral interpretation, lower luminosity makes the object colder and does not remove a pre-existing deficit, whereas a credible higher luminosity could weaken or remove it. The size or even presence of tension depends on the cooling model, age and physical interpretation; a distance-dependent change in inferred age or spectral component must also be considered jointly.

Short-distance alternative, not included in the box. Chen, Wang & Chen (2025) infer 0.948 ± 0.001 kpc from a Gaia extinction-profile fit, with the quoted uncertainty statistical only; systematic and extinction-feature association uncertainties remain. This estimate concerns the remnant, not a direct pulsar parallax, and the pulsar-remnant association remains an assumption. It adds support to the nearby H I scenario of Yar-Uyaniker et al. A lower near-distance luminosity can strengthen an existing discrepancy at a fixed age, but changes in age, fit and physical component can alter the comparison.

Age evidence: Suzuki et al. (2021) report a pulsar kinematic lower limit t > 19.42 kyr using the geometrical shell centre and proper motion. It is conditional on that centre representing the explosion position; the authors note that a possible centre offset can remove a robust constraint for this object. It is not a measured 19.42 kyr age or a hard lower bound. Do not count 22.7 kyr as an independently validated constraint until resolved.

Keep L∞<3.5 × 10³¹ erg/s and the previous 50 kyr plotting marker. No new central age, luminosity fit or confidence level is assigned.

Shternin et al. 2019, Shternin2019, favour a roughly 50 kyr birthplace age from measured proper motion and the RCW 114 association. The catalogue uses 40–60 kyr; the characteristic age is 80.5 kyr. Birthplace geometry and the association are assumptions, not an exact clock. Their remnant/distance argument differs from using an electron-density model to convert dispersion measure into distance.

P20, object 48, Potekhin2020, reports L∞<3.5 × 10³¹ erg/s and kT∞<33 eV. The NSMAXG+PL analysis fixed d=0.75 kpc and R=12.5 km. Its cited spectral work, Zyuzin, Karpova & Shibanov, was listed as in preparation. Thus this is a published compilation constraint with incompletely accessible primary-fit provenance; it must not be called an independently reproduced atmosphere limit. The current narrow WEB distance interval is not evidence that this spectral fit was repeated at that distance. Retain the original luminosity upper-limit arrow with the adopted 40–80 kyr horizontal extent; no symmetric luminosity error bar, positive lower bound or adopted expanded ceiling is introduced.

Open issues: retrieve the complete spectral analysis and confidence construction; check mass, atmosphere field, absorption, emitting-area and distance assumptions. Distances from a hypothetical rejected association must not be combined with the association-dependent age as though independent.
